Exactly as predicted: Go's case-insensitive struct field matching let 
`"ACTION": "getcosmic"` silently override the earlier `"action": "getSecureCode"` when populating `requestData.Action`, 
so the switch took the `getcosmic` branch and forwarded the *entire raw body* — including the 
still-present lowercase `"action": "getSecureCode"` key — to Flask untouched. Flask's strictly case-sensitive dict lookup then 
read that original lowercase key and handed back the flag.

The root cause in one line: **the Go proxy authorizes based on one interpretation of the JSON, but forwards the raw bytes for a 
completely different service to reinterpret** — any place two parsers can disagree on the meaning of the same bytes 
(case folding, duplicate keys, encoding, whitespace) is a potential authorization bypass whenever that gap exists between 
a decision point and an enforcement point. Good find.
